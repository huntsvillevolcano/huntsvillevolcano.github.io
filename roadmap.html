07 / SAFETY, FEASIBILITY & THE ROAD AHEAD

Big ambition.
Bigger questions.

The science does not yet exist to build a controlled volcano. The first milestone is not construction. It is finding out what is possible — and what should never be attempted.

A RESPONSIBLE ROADMAP

Let evidence set the pace. Each stage must earn the right to proceed.

Start with the reality

A full-scale constructed volcano is far beyond today's demonstrated engineering capabilities. The required science, materials, energy systems, containment methods and operating safeguards have not been established. This site describes a speculative vision, not a viable construction proposal.

Safety questions come first

  • Thermal hazards: Extreme heat, thermal shock, fires and equipment failure.
  • Gas and air quality: Potentially hazardous gases, particulates and emissions.
  • Containment: Structural failure, molten material migration and unexpected fluid pathways.
  • Water and geology: Groundwater interactions, induced seismicity, subsidence and contamination.
  • People and communities: Worker protection, emergency planning, exclusion zones and transparent risk communication.
  • Environmental impacts: Water use, energy demand, emissions, waste, land disturbance and long-term site stewardship.

A staged research roadmap

01

Independent scoping

Convene geoscientists, geothermal engineers, materials experts, safety professionals, regulators, educators and community representatives. Define the questions and identify disqualifying risks.

02

Fundamental laboratory research

Study heat transfer, rock mechanics, high-temperature materials, fluids, gas transport, sensors and containment in small, controlled experiments.

03

Component demonstrations

Test drilling, cooling, refractory materials, monitoring and emergency shutdown systems separately. Publish methods, failures and results.

04

Independent safety and environmental review

Evaluate hazards, alternatives, lifecycle impacts, permitting needs and public-interest value. Provide meaningful opportunities for public scrutiny.

05

Decision gate — proceed, redesign or stop

Advance only if evidence supports safety, scientific value, environmental responsibility and a credible economic case. Stopping is a valid and responsible outcome.

What this project is — and is not

It is a proposal to explore a bold research question inspired by Huntsville's history of technical ambition. It is not a claim that we can create, control or switch off natural volcanoes. Better experimental understanding might contribute to improved forecasting and hazard reduction, but natural systems remain complex and uncertain.

Our first milestone: Bring people together to define the science and test the assumptions. No construction decision should be made before the fundamental feasibility and safety questions are answered.
